===============================================================================
ApoorvCTF 2026 - Author on the Run Writeup
Category: Forensics
Points: 500
===============================================================================

CHALLENGE DESCRIPTION:
----------------------
An author was recording themselves typing keystrokes. They first recorded all
26 lowercase letters (a-z) 50 times each for a total of 1300 keypresses in
Reference.wav, then recorded themselves typing the flag into CTFd in flag.wav.
Recover the flag from the audio.

Flag Format: apoorvctf{flag}

FILES PROVIDED:
--------------
- Reference.wav  (26 MB, ~305 seconds — 1300 labeled keypresses, a-z x50 each)
- flag.wav       (1.1 MB, ~12 seconds — someone typing the flag)


SOLUTION:
---------

1. INITIAL RECONNAISSANCE

   Loaded both files at SR=44100 Hz (mono). Reference.wav contained 1300
   keypresses in strict alphabetical order: 50 presses of 'a', then 50 of 'b',
   etc. flag.wav had a short burst of keypresses to decode.

   Quick sanity check: 26 keys × 50 presses = 1300 expected onsets.


2. ONSET DETECTION

   librosa's built-in onset_detect failed completely — it merged or split
   clusters incorrectly. A custom energy-based detector was implemented:

   - Split audio into 5ms chunks and compute RMS energy per chunk
   - Smooth with uniform_filter1d to reduce transient spikes
   - Threshold = mean + 2.0 × std of the smoothed energy curve
   - Detect rising edges; enforce minimum 120ms gap between onsets

   This found exactly 1303 onsets in Reference.wav (target: 1300).
   The 3 extra onsets were absorbed by taking the first 1300.
   On flag.wav it found 21 onsets, not 19 or 20 as initially expected.


3. FEATURE EXTRACTION AND CLASSIFIER TRAINING

   For each onset, a window of 80ms starting at the onset was extracted.
   Features: 20 MFCCs + delta + delta-delta = 120-dimensional vector
             (mean and std over time frames → 6 × 20 = 120 dims)

   Ground-truth labels for Reference.wav: onset i maps to letter
       KEY_ORDER[i * 26 // 1300]
   where KEY_ORDER = list('qwertyuiopasdfghjklzxcvbnm').

   Three classifiers trained:
   - k-Nearest Neighbors (k=7, distance-weighted)
   - SVM (RBF kernel, C=10)
   - Random Forest (300 estimators)
   All features StandardScaler-normalized.


4. FIRST PASS DECODING

   All three models unanimously agreed on the first 13 characters:
       POSITIONS 1-13: o h y o u g o t t h i s f

   Positions 14-21 showed conflicts. Key observation: onsets at positions 15
   and 19 were only ~140ms after positions 14 and 18 respectively, while all
   other inter-keypress gaps were 400-900ms. Something unusual was happening.


5. THE KEY-RELEASE ECHO PROBLEM

   Detailed per-onset interval analysis revealed that every keypress in
   flag.wav actually produces TWO rising edges in the energy signal:

   - Press event:    the keypress — primary onset
   - Release event:  the key release ~66-78ms later — normally filtered out
                     by the 100ms minimum gap, but...

   Positions 14 and 18 had their secondary event (key release) arriving at
   exactly ~138ms — just above the 100ms threshold. So the energy detector
   mistakenly captured these release events as separate keypresses, giving
   21 onsets instead of the true 19 real keypresses.

   CONCLUSION: Positions 15 and 19 in the 21-onset list are key-release echoes
   of positions 14 and 18. The real flag is 19 characters long.


6. SPECTRAL ANALYSIS TO DISAMBIGUATE

   With echoes removed, the flag structure was:
       ohyougotthisf [X] rd [Y] mn
   where X = onset index 13, Y = onset index 17 (release echoes discarded).

   Positions 16-17 (r,d) and 20-21 (m,n) were confirmed by all models.
   X and Y remained uncertain.

   Spectral template matching was applied:
   - For each reference key, compute the mean magnitude spectrum (Hanning
     windowed FFT, L1-normalized) over all 50 training presses
   - Compute cosine similarity between each flag onset's spectrum and every
     key template

   Result:
   - X = 'a' (cosine similarity 0.8734 — closest template)
   - Y = 'a' (cosine similarity 0.8751 — closest template)

   MFCC-based kNN found X = 'g', Y = 'w' at low confidence (~0.55), but
   spectral matching outperformed kNN on these acoustically unusual presses
   because the high-frequency impact content was the discriminating feature.


7. FLAG RECOVERY

   Substituting X=Y='a' into the 19-character decoded string:

       ohyougotthisf + a + rd + a + mn  =  ohyougotthisfardamn

   Reading it aloud: "oh you got this far, damn!"  — the author's cheeky
   challenge completion message.

   Final flag: apoorvctf{ohyougotthisfardamn}


TOOLS USED:
-----------
- Python 3 with librosa, numpy, scipy, scikit-learn
- Custom energy-based onset detector (5ms RMS chunks, uniform smoothing)
- MFCC feature extraction (20 coefficients + delta + delta-delta)
- kNN / SVM / Random Forest ensemble
- Spectral cosine template matching (Hanning FFT, L1-normalized)


KEY INSIGHT:
------------
The two "extra" onsets (positions 15 and 19) were key-release echoes, not
additional keypresses. Once removed, the remaining 19 onsets decoded cleanly
with high confidence on 17 of 19 characters. The final 2 uncertain characters
were resolved by spectral template matching (cosine similarity on magnitude
spectra), which gave 'a' for both — resulting in the readable phrase
"oh you got this far, damn!"


FLAG: apoorvctf{ohyougotthisfardamn}
